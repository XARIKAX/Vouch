The stack

A router picks a vendor.
Vouch guarantees the result.

Routing layers broadcast your call, take the winning bid, and settle it — whatever comes back. That's the floor. Vouch sits one layer up: the same auction, then escrow, verification, and slashable stake standing between delivery and payment.

01

Discovery & routing

Broadcast a request, collect vendor bids on price, latency, and reputation, route to the winner. Fast — but it pays out on delivery, not on correctness.

where routers stop
02

Metered settlement

Money moves for every call that returns a response. A response that's wrong still cleared — detecting and re-paying is your problem.

still just calls
03

Verified outcomes

Committed quotes bonded by stake. Escrow locks at the quoted price. Output is checked against your acceptance criteria — and only a full pass releases payment. Failure refunds you and slashes the provider.

✓ Table stakes — matched
  • MCP-native — one endpoint, three lines of config; agents drive the whole loop as tools.
  • USDC settlement — fund escrow directly; no bank, no per-vendor billing relationship.
  • Zero API keys — one Vouch key reaches the entire provider network.
  • Sealed vendor auctions — providers compete on price, deadline, and track record every task.
  • On-chain-style receipts — every lock, settlement, refund, and slash carries a transaction hash.
★ The Vouch layer — only here
  • ★Committed quotes — the price at dispatch is the price at settlement. No metered drift, no surprise clearing price.
  • ★Verification gates payment — schema, deterministic checks, a three-judge rubric panel, or your webhook. Nothing settles without a full pass.
  • ★Slashable stake — capital is bonded to every quote. Ship junk and the provider loses money, not you.
  • ★Automatic refunds — failed work returns your escrow with no dispute to file and no call to re-pay.
  • ★24-hour recourse — dispute a settled task and an independent, stricter panel re-reviews it.
